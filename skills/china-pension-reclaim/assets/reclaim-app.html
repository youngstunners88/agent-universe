Unclaimed in China

If you worked in China on a work permit and left without closing your social-insurance account, the 8% taken off every payslip is still sitting there in your name. It does not expire, it earns interest, and you can take it as a lump sum from anywhere in the world. Most people never find out.

待办PENDING
The short answer 结论

Yes — but the first job is proving the account exists at all.

The case below is a South African ESL teacher who worked in Shanghai until 2022, whose employer has since been dissolved, and who has no termination letter. Every one of those facts is survivable. Change the inputs and the whole kit re-writes itself for your situation.

Can he claim from abroad?
Yes
No bilateral treaty is needed to withdraw. Leaving China permanently is itself the qualifying event.
Must he fly to Shanghai?
No
Shanghai is a counter-only service, but it accepts an agent acting on a signed power of attorney. The agent needs a PRC ID card.
What does the government charge?
Nothing
不收费 — the filing is free. Your cost is notarising and apostilling the paperwork.
Is it too late?
No
The individual account has no claim deadline. Balances from 2022 are still there, still accruing.
Step 00 · What is in there 个人账户储存额估算

Estimate the balance before you spend a cent on paperwork

Chinese pension has two buckets. Your 8% goes to a personal account with your name on it — that is refundable. The employer's 16% goes to the city pooling fund and is gone the moment you stop being a Chinese pensioner-in-waiting. Knowing the number tells you whether the notary fees are worth it.

Step 01 · The phone call 先打电话确认

One call settles whether you have a claim

Do this before paying any agent. The operator can look you up on your passport number alone and tell you, on the spot, whether an account exists and what is in it. Shanghai's 12345 line will transfer you to an English desk if you speak English — say so immediately and wait through the Chinese prompt.

Read before you start 风险提示

  
The four things that actually derail these claims

  

    
Your Chinese bank account is closed
This is the most common failure. The payout goes to a Chinese account in your own name — not your agent's. If yours was closed, reopening it usually requires you in person, which defeats the whole plan. Ask the operator on the first call whether the branch will remit overseas or accept a designated account; policy varies by branch and has been loosening.

    
You may never have been enrolled
Shanghai only made social insurance compulsory for foreigners on 16 August 2021. Before that, employers could opt you out, and training centres routinely did. If you taught at a tutoring centre killed by the July 2021 crackdown, your enrolment window may be a handful of months — or zero. The call decides it.

    
Withdrawing is one-way
Taking the lump sum terminates the relationship and zeroes your contribution history. If you might work in China again, the alternative is to leave the account dormant — it keeps earning interest and resumes accumulating when you return. Only 15 years of contributions ever unlocks an actual monthly pension.

    
The dissolved employer is not fatal
When a company deregisters, it closes its social-insurance registration and your status drops to terminated automatically — which is the proof the counter wants. Your exit stamps and expired work permit corroborate it. Bring the statement of circumstances and let the branch check the registry.

  






  
What this is. A working kit, not legal advice. Rules are set nationally by the Interim Measures for the Participation in Social Insurance of Foreigners Employed in China (MOHRSS Order No. 16, 2011) but administered branch by branch, and counter practice moves faster than published guidance. Verify the document list on the call before you spend money on notarisation.
