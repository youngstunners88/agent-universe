# Case brief

South African national, ESL teacher.

- Started work in Shanghai **July 2018**.
- **Physically left China August 2022** — has not returned.
- Employment formally continued **remotely from South Africa** until **February 2023**.
- Gross salary **RMB 16,000/month**.
- Employer has ceased operations and been deregistered. No termination letter obtainable.
- Currently in South Africa. Does not speak Chinese. Limited budget for fees.
- Wants to know: can he claim, who does he contact, what will it cost, must he appear
  physically, and how long will it take.

## Facts the tool must handle correctly

- Shanghai only made social insurance compulsory for foreign employees on **16 Aug 2021**.
  Before that, employers could opt foreigners out under 沪人社养发〔2009〕38号, and most did.
  If he WAS voluntarily enrolled pre-2021, that scheme's contribution base was pegged near
  60% of the prior year's citywide average wage, not his actual salary.
- The months worked remotely from South Africa (Aug 2022 – Feb 2023) are almost certainly
  NOT contributing months: social insurance attaches to employment *within* China under a
  valid work permit. His likely contributing window is therefore Aug 2021 – Aug 2022.
- South Africa has no bilateral social-security treaty with China, so no exemption applied.
- Only the employee's 8% pension and 2% medical personal accounts are recoverable. The
  employer's 16% goes to the pooling fund and is not.
- Shanghai processes this at the counter only; an agent needs their own PRC resident ID card.
- China joined the Apostille Convention on 7 Nov 2023; SA documents are apostilled by DIRCO,
  no longer legalised by the Chinese Embassy.

The audience is not only this one person — the tool is meant to serve any former foreign
worker in China who has forgotten this money exists.
